# Listening guide

All 24 fugues and all 24 preludes now exist.
Nobody involved in writing them has heard a note; every check was made on
the score. Your ears are the first real test.

Play them in the [listening room](https://claude.ai/artifact/BBJMS7b46MiEycNBM24yhB)
(any tuning, solo or mute any voice) or from the MP3s in each pair's
`renders/` folder. Fugues V–XXIV also have `fugue-spatial.mp3`, a binaural
render that places each voice around you; use headphones. (Fugues I–IV and
the preludes have no binaural version yet.)

## If you have half an hour

These are the pieces whose idea should be audible on a first hearing:

1. **Pair I** (C major): the prelude climbs through all 24 keys and never
   arrives (5 min together).
2. **Fugue XVII** (A♭ major): every cadence dodged until the last voice
   resolves the children's hanging chord (4 min).
3. **Fugue XX** (A minor): one violin line splits into 21 voices, then folds
   back into one violin (4 min).
4. **Fugue XXII** (B♭ minor): the second half is the first half backwards;
   listen for the opening returning in reverse (4½ min).
5. **Pair XXIV** (B minor): the prelude walks through all 24 keys quoting
   Bach's Book I subjects, then the finale for twenty-five parts (6½ min).

## What each pair is trying to do

Voices in brackets. Since October 2026 each fugue has at most one voice more
than Bach's fugue in the same key; XX, XXI and XXIV keep their crowds.

| Pair | Listen for | Known weak spot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I C (2) | the endless rise; one subject at every stretto distance | keys in bars 8–17 pass by the same pivot; the 2- and 1-eighth strettos may not read as entries |
| II C minor (3) | the whole trio turned upside down; Bach's subject as a guest | the build (b25–26) is the least thematic stretch |
| III C♯ (4) | the gigue and its mirror sounding at once in the coda | each subject entry opens on a bar of plain tonic |
| IV C♯ minor (5) | Bach's "cross" subject against ours | more homorhythmic than Bach; some held basses need the sostenuto pedal |
| V D (5) | the Guest voice singing only Bach's subject, into silence; entries stepping down through four keys | the subject's repeated notes; the ×½ at b46 is on paper only |
| VI D minor (4) | the Crucifixus lament climbing from bass to soprano; two speeds at once at the summit | every station follows the same plan |
| VII E♭ (4) | two choirs of two quarrelling about the key; Coro I's pomp against Coro II's chatter, and the alto stealing Coro I's subject at double speed (b28) | long two-voice opening |
| VIII E♭ minor (4) | the slow voice in the bass; six stretto entries, one at a time, over the subject at quarter speed (b27–32); Bach's subject alone near the end | two inner stretto entries may blur (alto, b27.4 and b30) |
| IX E (4) | four singers in a ring (binaural); Bach's E-major subject as guest and as the final cantus | a thin middle: at most three voices from b21, only duets in b33–46 |
| X E minor (3) | one voice per choir in dialogue under the chorale *O Lamm Gottes* | Coro I very busy |
| XI F (4) | the trumpet stuck in the wrong key (b38–44) | leapy (trumpet's repeated notes) |
| XII F minor (5) | a conjectural completion of the Art of Fugue's unfinished fugue; the fifth voice entering with the main theme | the four subjects move together over the pedal for only two bars (b59.3–61.3) |
| XIII F♯ (4) | canons at rising intervals over the Goldberg bass; the folk-song quodlibet | the close canons cross (b5–6, b14) and are told apart only by rhythm; a low, quiet ending |
| XIV F♯ minor (5) | the Royal Theme in a canon that climbs C, D, E minor and arrives home | a long, thin opening (b1–21, one to three voices); the F♯6 peak comes late, inside the ritardando (b75) |
| XV G (4) | the four lines of *Vom Himmel hoch* together, one per voice (b43–48) | the summit is short and slow: all four lines sound together for only ten beats of half notes (b44–47) |
| XVI G minor (5) | each chorale line in canon between two pedal voices; Bach's G-minor organ fugue on top before the summit | the last canon sounds like an octave doubling |
| XVII A♭ (5) | fourteen dodged cadences; the father's entry and first real cadence | evasions 1–9 are all the same kind (V → vi/VI), and in b16 the four voices move as chords |
| XVIII G♯ minor (5) | one note brightening per night watch; the knocker; the dawn in the tonic major (written A♭) | the dawn is short |
| XIX A (4) | four dances in four metres at once; Bach's Bourrée revealed at the end | the middle is mostly two voices |
| XX A minor (21) | voices splitting out of one violin line; a crescendo into the summit, where the strings thin while the double basses' lament descends, then all 21 land together on the dominant (bar 46) | the folds back into one violin may be hard to follow |
| XXI B♭ (22) | the horn "cracking" to its flat 7th harmonic; the friends turning to F, the horns' key (bar 20); the final natural-harmonic chord | leapy (posthorn idiom); the friends' section is quiet and thin by design |
| XXII B♭ minor (6) | the palindrome: B-A-C-H before the axis, H-C-A-B after it | the second half's harmony is softer-edged and wandering (its passing notes land on the beats) |
| XXIII B (5) | Bull's six-note scale through all twelve keys; meantone at bar 69 (or the short demo files); the one double-time statement (bars 75–78) that brings B back early | the six-bar clock is regular until bar 75 |
| XXIV B minor (25 parts) | the 24-key walk; Bach's own B-minor subject on top at bar 11; the cycle's subjects arriving over the slow bass; Fugue I's ladder climbing from the basses | at most 14 real lines at once; all 25 only on the last chord |

## The most useful feedback

- Which pieces you would keep, and which you would cut or redo.
- Where something sounds like a mistake (bar numbers or timings).
- Whether the big ideas (palindrome, splitting violin, four dances, natural
  harmonics, dodged cadences) can be heard, or only read about.
- Whether the new, smaller fugues are clear enough, and whether any should
  be bigger again.
- Lengths: too long, too short, about right.
