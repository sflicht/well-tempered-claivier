# Listening guide

All 24 fugues and 21 of the 24 preludes now exist: about two hours of music.
Nobody involved in writing them has heard a note; every check was made on
the score. Your ears are the first real test.

Play them in the [listening room](https://claude.ai/artifact/BBJMS7b46MiEycNBM24yhB)
(any tuning, solo or mute any voice) or from the MP3s in each pair's
`renders/` folder. `fugue-spatial.mp3` places the voices around you; use
headphones.

## If you have half an hour

These are the pieces whose idea should be audible on a first hearing:

1. **Pair I** (C major): the prelude climbs through all 24 keys and never
   arrives (5 min together).
2. **Fugue XVII** (A♭ major): every cadence dodged until the last voice
   resolves the children's hanging chord (4 min).
3. **Fugue XX** (A minor): one violin line splits into 21 voices, then folds
   back into one violin (4 min).
4. **Fugue XXII** (B♭ minor): the second half is the first half backwards;
   listen for the opening returning in reverse (4½ min).
5. **Pair XXIV** (B minor): the prelude walks through all 24 keys quoting
   Bach's Book I subjects, then the 25-voice finale (6½ min).

## The most useful feedback

- Which pieces you would keep, and which you would cut or redo.
- Where something sounds like a mistake (bar numbers or timings).
- Whether the big ideas (palindrome, splitting violin, four dances, natural
  harmonics, dodged cadences) can be heard, or only read about.
- Whether the many-voice fugues sound like counterpoint or like harmony.
- Lengths: too long, too short, about right.
