NEXT COMPLETE COMPOSITION: FUGUE IV IN C-SHARP MINOR
Compositional brief prepared 23 October 2026.
Status: proposed subject and form, not a completed or verified score.

PURPOSE
Give the cycle a different rhythmic and dramatic personality after VIII:
a quick 6/8 gigue whose momentum comes from interruptions and displaced
emphasis. Remain in minor at the end. Do not recycle VIII's slow chant,
4/4 dance, or minor-to-major apotheosis.

SUBJECT CANDIDATE A (RECOMMENDED)
Durations are quarter-note beats. Each bar contains 3 quarter-note beats.
Bar 1: C#4 .5; E4 .5; G#4 1; F#4 .5; E4 .5.
Bar 2: D#4 .5; B#3 .5; C#4 1; G#3 .5; rest .5.

The long note begins before the second dotted-quarter pulse and crosses it.
A rising broken triad, the displacement, and the final falling fourth
supply three related ingredients for episodes. The range is one octave.

PROPOSED REAL DOMINANT ANSWER
Bar 1: G#4 .5; B4 .5; D#5 1; C#5 .5; B4 .5.
Bar 2: A#4 .5; F-double-sharp4 .5; G#4 1; D#4 .5; rest .5.

The accompaniment must follow the temporary dominant region and return
purposefully to C#; it cannot merely repeat tonic-minor chord notes.
A one-bar stretto has not been established. B# in the subject may conflict
with B-natural in the answer. Compose each overlap before claiming it works.

ALTERNATIVE B (NOT SELECTED)
Broader 3/2 question, durations also in quarter beats:
Bar 1: C#4 2; B#3 1; C#4 1; E4 2.
Bar 2: D#4 1.5; F#4 .5; E4 1; D#4 1; G#3 2.
Its slow-theme/active-accompaniment risk is too close to VIII for this slot.

RECOMMENDED FORM
Dotted quarter=72 (quarter=108 in the present renderer).
120 bars of 6/8 =360 quarter beats =200 seconds, before final resonance.

Bars 1–20: five entries and real continuations, with breaths that let each
new entry register. Proposed order: tenor, upper alto, bass, soprano,
remaining middle voice.
21–36: argumentative pairs; descending-fourth episodes and displaced
pulse; A major toward E major.
37–52: warmer E-major passage with an actual major-mode transformation,
not arbitrary note transposition.
53–72: F#-minor disturbance; occasional three-quarter-note accents against
the compound pulse, released at a meaningful cadence.
73–84: quieter exchange with moving bass; incomplete replies approach G#.
85–108: C#-minor return; augmented subject, quicker thematic statement,
and three individually shaped counterlines. Stretto only where the
written combination proves coherent.
109–120: energy unwinds through continuing counterpoint; minor cadence
with staggered resolutions rather than a long block-chord finish.

FIVE ROLES, TO ROTATE BETWEEN PARTS
Soprano working range G#4–E6: long arcs and held tensions.
Upper alto E4–C#6: displaced accents and late resolutions.
Middle C#4–G#5: clipped replies, short sequences, audible breaths.
Tenor G#3–E5: first statement, later larger leaps and answering gestures.
Bass C#2–C#4: thematic movement, changing pace, late augmentation.
These are flexible working ranges, not rigid pitch lanes. No voice should
be assigned a permanent pad or accompaniment function.

FIRST COMPOSITIONAL TASK
Write the subject–answer duet and two contrasting countersubjects, then
compose the five-entry exposition and a candidate late combination.
Inspect those actual combinations before extending the complete form.
This brief does not establish their consonance, memorability, or sound.
