FUGUE I — RECOMPOSITION STUDY

A new two-part composition in C major, 25 bars of 4/4, quarter = 116.
Written duration: 51.724 seconds. Exactly two monophonic lines.

WHY THE PREVIOUS IDEA WAS REJECTED
The old subject began C-E-G in quarters and returned to a sustained tonic
within two bars. Its self-contained tonic excursion encouraged complete
little blocks connected by externally supplied episodes. Adding notes to
that outline did not supply a strong upbeat or a reason to continue.

THE NEW MUSICAL IDEA
After an opening eighth rest: C5 eighth, E5 eighth, D5-C5 sixteenths,
B4-C5-G4-A4 eighths; B4-C5-D5-G4 eighths, E5 dotted eighth, D5 sixteenth,
C5-B4 eighths. Its final B resolves into the next phrase, not within a
closed subject. The rising third and quick recoil recur in the episodes;
the G-A-B-C-D ascent supplies the final cadential tail.

The companion crosses several of the subject's moving notes with longer
held notes. It is actually exchanged above and below the subject, in the
G-major entry and the tonic return. The weak fifths that turn into fourths
when the parts exchange are passing notes, rather than accented pillars.
The complete recurring companion core begins with the subject's first
played upbeat. The half-beat before it is a free connection appropriate
to the previous phrase; it is not counted as an exact thematic statement.

FORM AND HARMONIC DIRECTION
Bars 1–4: upper C subject; real G answer below, companion above. The
upper subject's B moves into C while the answer approaches its G upbeat.

Bars 5–8: the recoil and rising tail lead into a G-major upper statement,
now with the same companion below. The two parts exchange their functions.

Bars 9–12: a low bass head sequence rises from G to A. Its final F-sharp
continues through G-sharp into the A-minor subject. The minor version has
a flattened third and the ascending form of degrees 6 and 7. Its raised
leading note is essential to the continuing line.

Bars 13–16: the head moves through A, F and D. The third stage passes
into the lower part; the upper part becomes a longer-note partner. A
chromatic resolution C-sharp/G to D/F-sharp leads into the dominant route
back to C. This is one continuous development rather than a set of three
new full-subject entries.

Bars 17–20: C returns first in the lower part while the companion makes
its largest registral arch. The upper statement and lower companion then
complete the exchange. Each subject's terminal leading note again moves
onward to the tonic in its own line.

Bars 21–25: the head starts half a beat earlier, then passes down under
an upper B held across the barline. The cadence recalls the head's recoil
and turns F-F-sharp-G toward the dominant. Over the final G, the ascending
tail and held B receive the concluding C withheld by complete statements.

WHAT IS NOT CLAIMED
There is no claimed stretto or 25-part achievement here. There are six
complete subject/answer statements and five complete companion cores.
No old cycle notes or generated chord-plan passages were reused. The
Python source encodes literal authored phrase pairs and transpositions of
recurring thematic material; no constraint solver chose the pitches.

The technical review is a check for errors, not evidence of rhythmic zest
or beauty. The work still uses conventional tonal sequences and is a
compact audition study. Its exact phrase energy and whether the subject
is memorable must be judged by listening. No auditory evaluation is
claimed in this session.
