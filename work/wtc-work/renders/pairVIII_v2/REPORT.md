# Pair VIII v2 — Prelude (v1, unchanged) + Fugue in E-flat minor (v2)

Files: `prelude-viii_v1.*` (copied untouched), `fugue-viii_v2.{mp3,pdf,mid,json,musicxml}`, `checks/` (checker output, parallel diagnosis, generator scripts).
Andante maestoso, q=66, 4/4, 32 bars, nine-voice palette used sparingly as *registers* (most of the piece is 2–4 voices).

## Material
- **Subject (1 bar + 1 beat):** Eb–Bb (rising 5th, quarters) | Cb(dotted 8th)–Bb(16th) sigh, Ab–Gb | F–D (leading tone), Eb. Implied harmony i–iv–V–i: one clear cadence inside the theme.
- **Tonal answer:** Bb–Eb–Gb… ending on Bb (v), the 5th answered by a 4th.
- **Countersubject:** starts *off* the beat after an 8th rest with 16ths (Bb Ab), then a long falling "sigh" scale in 8ths and quarters — moves where the subject holds, holds where the subject moves. It has its own descending shape, the opposite of the subject's leap upward, so it's easy to pick out and remember.

## Beethoven-style devices (kept simple)
1. Register as orchestration: entries are distributed by register (alto, soprano, tenor, high soprano, bass) rather than by voice count.
2. The climax is built from harmony and register: a Neapolitan chord, a half-diminished ii, then V7, with the subject in octaves over chordal massing.
3. A deceptive cadence to Cb (bVI) followed straight away by a subito-pp return of the subject in that remote major key, thinly scored.
4. A tonic pedal under the last stretto, so the tightest overlap happens over stable harmony.
5. No canons, inversions or augmentations. Only straight entries, done cleanly.

## Honest self-critique
- I can't listen to the audio. These judgements come from analysis and the checker, not from hearing it.
- The free voices are generated by a beam search over a chord plan written by hand. The structure, cadences and thematic placement are deliberate, but the filler counterpoint is competent at best, and it still has 18 free-voice parallels and a couple of unprepared clashes (b19.1, b23.3).
- The tertian fit is 67%, so in places the inner voices still blur the harmony, especially in the dense bars 20–24 (48–74 attacks per bar). The thinner sections (exposition, Cb passage) should sound cleaner than the strettos.
- The Cb-major return goes straight back to E-flat minor without a dominant pivot. I removed the earlier V pedal to keep it simple, but this could sound abrupt instead of surprising.
- Stretto II at 3 beats has two weak vertical spots that I accepted.
