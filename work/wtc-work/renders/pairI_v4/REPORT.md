# Fugue I in C minor, v4 (two voices, 34 bars, about 2:01, Andante con moto, quarter = 69)

The prelude is unchanged: prelude-i_v3.* is copied here for pairing.

## New subject (2 bars)
`C5:4 Eb5:2 D5:2 C5:2 B4:2 C5:4 | Ab5:4 G5:2 Gb5:1 F5:1 Eb5:2 D5:2 C5:4`
- Bar 1 is a tonic motto that circles C with a leading-tone turn (C–Eb–D–C–B–C), long-short-short.
- Bar 2 opens with the hook, a minor-6th leap up to Ab. A lament-style chromatic slide (G–Gb–F) then runs down to the tonic. This slide is the only chromatic colour.
- The answer is real, at the dominant (G minor).
- The countersubject is diatonic: a held 5th, then a falling scale with a dotted figure and a cadential turn. It returns in the relative major.

## Form
- **b1–4:** exposition (subject, then answer with countersubject).
- **b5–6:** circle-of-fifths episode to Eb major.
- **b7–10:** subject and countersubject in Eb.
- **b11–12:** circle of fifths towards F minor.
- **b13–16:** augmentation canon in F minor (subdominant).
- **b17–18:** episode.
- **b19–24:** inversion canon in G minor, wide range.
- **b25–26:** hand-written build over a falling bass.
- **b27–28:** peak. The subject is answered two octaves below, a quarter later.
- **b29–30:** Neapolitan Db, then a dominant bariolage.
- **b31–32:** final stretto over a C2 pedal.
- **b33–34:** perfect cadence and a short minor close. No Picardy third, and no pedal at the end.

## Checks (checks/f4.txt)
In v3 there were dozens of flags. v4 has:
- 1 unprepared strong-beat dissonance (b13.4).
- 3 brief m2/M7 passing clashes (b22, b31).
- 1 sixteenth-grid parallel (b7.3).
- No eighth-grid parallel 5ths or octaves.
- A few false relations (F against F#, from the melodic-minor answer).
- The inversion canon briefly crosses voices in b22–23.

## Honest critique
- The subject is far more tonal and memorable, but the rhythm of bar 1 is a little square.
- The free counterpoint generated in the episodes and canons is still filler rather than motivic. Episodes don't quote the countersubject much.
- The inversion canon (b19–24) is the most "academic" stretch and the least tuneful.
- The peak stretto works only because the leader is adjusted (Ab held for 6, F–G instead of Gb–F). This means the chromatic hook is missing at the climax.
- The countersubject was written over the answer and doesn't invert cleanly, so in Eb it is placed above the subject only.
