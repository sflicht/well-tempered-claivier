Use a 60-bar form: 240 quarter beats at quarter=108 gives 133.333 seconds
of music, about 2:16 with the present audio tail. This removes 40 percent
of the current 100-bar form while preserving the new retort paragraph.
The cuts require composed joins; the coordinates below are a structural
map, not permission to concatenate arbitrary MIDI slices.

Do not paste old72’s bass C-flat under the surviving old55 material without

JOIN AT NEW112 / EIGHT-BAR EPISODE

The clarinet’s old124–132 major rising theme becomes new108–116 and crosses
the proposed new episode boundary. Preserve or deliberately recompose the
whole statement; do not truncate it and keep an exact-entry annotation.
Its second bar (new112–116) contains D-flat, C-flat, A-flat, F: these are
natural members of D-flat seventh. That suggests the following compact
bass/harmony route, one bar each, with passing motion within each bar:

New112–116: D-flat7, continuing the clarinet phrase.
New116–120: G-flat, a brief relative-major confirmation.
New120–124: F7, an applied dominant with purposeful A-natural.
New124–128: B-flat minor, reached through A-natural -> B-flat.
New128–132: E-flat7, with G-natural directed to A-flat.
New132–136: A-flat minor, the minor subdominant.
New136–140: F half-diminished (F, A-flat, C-flat, E-flat), predominant.
New140–144: B-flat7, directed to E-flat minor at new144.

This route gives named destinations rather than a long drift through notes
from one scale. It is a plan for bass and voice-leading, not a chord grid
onto which six filler lines should be placed. Compose an imitative pair
from the retort or dance tail, a complementary moving bass, then add the
necessary independently shaped lines. Use four or five voices for much of
this episode; the absent voices earn their next entries. A quieter passage
must still move harmonically.

The oboe’s old126 C-flat and cello’s old126 D-flat finish at old128/new112.
Both can participate naturally in the new D-flat7 phrase. Avoid simultaneous
rearticulation of every voice at112. Let a phrase cross the boundary.

NEW FINAL SIXTEEN BARS:176–240

176–192 (bars45–48): a brief release after the returning dense paragraph.
Reduce to five or six moving voices, use an actual subdominant diversion,
and retain a short motif in imitation. Keep momentum; no new slow-memory
section or long reset.

192–208 (bars49–52): bring the absent voices back successively. Combine
one measured chant, one quick theme and distinct responding lines, with
entries composed against the entire sounding texture. This is the compact
nine-part culmination. Its apex should arise from voice-leading tension,
register and the contraction of entry intervals. Do not increase every
velocity or hold a nine-note sonority as the main event.

208–224 (bars53–56): a single directional predominant–dominant approach.
Possible harmonic functions: A-flat minor -> F half-diminished -> cadential
six-four over B-flat -> B-flat7. A changing bass in the first two bars and
at most two bars of dominant pedal avoids another long harmonic plateau.
Suspensions must be prepared and resolve in individual lines.

224–240 (bars57–60): tonic-minor arrival with motion inside the release,
followed by one short final cadence. Four bars is an upper limit, not an
invitation to keep restating the same chord. Let some voices finish early.
Stay in E-flat minor; if a Picardy third is wanted, reserve G-natural for
the final arrival only. Avoid a large E-flat-major victory section.

STYLE AND PERFORMANCE IMPLICATIONS

The main path toward the WTC inspiration is a better relation between
subject, bass movement, dissonance and cadence: purposeful sequences,
clear applied dominants, motivated episodes and compact form. Switching
to harpsichord or attaching Baroque terminology does not supply this.

Piano should be the primary audition. Use restrained foreground/background
voicing, short phrase-specific breaths and a coherent dynamic range.
Treat the nine written voices as lines whose roles change. A constant
loudness hierarchy by instrument would harden the same registral problems.
The chamber version can wait until the composition works in its primary
keyboard medium. No claim about the resulting sound is made in this report.
