INDEPENDENT SCORE REVIEW — NEW FUGUE I AND PRELUDE IV STUDIES

Scope: literal notes, harmonic direction, rhythmic identity, phrase joins
and continuing roles in the two new scores. This is score-based review;
I have not heard the audio. No numerical or interval checklist can establish
that these pieces are aesthetically successful, much less comparable to Bach.

FUGUE I — WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED

The new subject begins off the downbeat and contains a recognizable
eighth/sixteenth gesture: C–E–D–C–B–C, followed by a rising continuation.
Its final B is unresolved until the following C. That is a consequential
change from the old rounded tonic half-note ending: the subject leaves
unfinished melodic business that continues as the second voice enters.

The accompanying line has longer notes and a different accentual shape.
The two voices exchange material and initiative rather than separating
permanently into an animated upper line and a chord-confirming bass.
The episodes are now literal phrases written for their joins. The closing
head exchanges and continuing bass give a credible local reason to move
toward the final dominant pedal.

Two transcription/connection issues were identified during review and were
already corrected in the author's next state. The upper G-subject entry at
q24.5 had been an octave too low, producing a fourteen-semitone drop into
the entry and a thirteen-semitone recovery afterwards. Restoring its octave
preserves the phrase's register. At q59–60 the former G4→F#5 major-seventh
jump was replaced by G4→F#4. The current continuation is F#4–G4–D5–C5–B4–A4–
F#4–B4. Against the bass, F#4 resolves the earlier C#/G tension; the move to
G4 over C3 reaches a fifth by contrary motion; the following upward fifth
to D5 is answered by a descent. This join now makes melodic sense.

I find no further material literal-note objection in the reviewed final
state. The tonal route remains compact and conventional. The subject and
episodes show more deliberate rhythmic direction, but the piece is a modest
two-voice study; it has not yet demonstrated a long, accumulating expressive
argument or anything about the feasibility of twenty-five-part counterpoint.
Listening should decide whether its motor is lively and its repetitions
earn their place, rather than treating cleaner authorship as a quality award.

PRELUDE IV — WHAT ACTUALLY CHANGED

The new 3/4 prelude is founded on one recurring gesture, with sixteenth-unit
durations 2,1,1,2,3,1,1,1. Its long target begins halfway through beat 2,
continues across beat 3, then releases into a descending tail. The durations
therefore establish a local direction rather than being a stock fast surface.
The gesture moves into the bass in bars 5–6 and 17–18 while the upper voice
continues in longer values. These are real exchanges of musical initiative.

The final opening bass C#3–E3, F#3–G#3, A3–C#3 supplies an ascending arc
against the descending upper response. At bars 4, 16 and 24 a held upper
note is made into a seventh by the bass change and resolves down to a sixth.
Other ties at bars 9→10, 13→14 and 19→20 similarly join phrases through
prepared dissonance. The suspensions now do compositional work: one part
continues while the other changes the harmonic situation.

The earlier bar-12 harmonic problem was removed before this final review:
the D#–E#–F# lead articulates C# dominant and the approach to F# minor.
Bar 20 expands an ascent through several harmonies, regaining the opening
register before the tonic return. The later C#6 arrival is prepared by that
return and the ongoing gesture, rather than being the sole signal of climax.
The closing D#-rooted half-diminished implication and G# dominant pedal give
the final span a clear tonal destination.

I recommended one final phrase fix, adopted by the author. Bar 8 originally
ended D#4→G#4 into E major. The bass reached E, but the exposed upper leading
tone did not complete its own motion. Changing the final sixteenth to E4
gives C#4–D#4–F#4–E4, an anticipation of the arrival, before G#4 opens the
next cell. That small change improves the actual melodic join without
replacing the piece's rhythmic identity.

I find no further material literal-note or phrase-join fix in the final
reviewed state. The piece remains brief and substantially sequential, with
familiar harmonic paths. Its advance is specific: a gesture governs its
continuations, the bass sometimes owns that gesture, and suspensions and
registral movement connect phrases. Whether those means produce the desired
zest is now a question for audition. This study also gives no reason to
discard the distinct spaces and slower arcs the user likes in III and VIII.

DECISION

Both studies are suitable for a candid listening comparison as fresh,
limited compositions. Neither is a replacement cycle or a certification of
success. Preserve the user's positive response to the promising preludes;
rebuild the fugues' compositional method separately and from the ground up.
