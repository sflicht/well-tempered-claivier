FUGUE METHOD — FROM-SCRATCH REASSESSMENT

STATUS

This is a proposed compositional method and a diagnosis of the earlier work.
It is not a report of a successful nine- or twenty-five-part proof. No such
proof has been composed or established. The new two-part Fugue I remains a
small audition candidate, not evidence that the method scales.

DIAGNOSIS: WE OPTIMIZED THE WRONG MUSICAL OBJECT

The old system's primary object is a sequence of chord-compatible pitch
assignments, not a network of melodies whose encounters create the harmony.
That makes the user's "all the fugues sound the same" a structural criticism,
not a request for more varied ornament.

The source makes this explicit:

- In tools/compose_cycle_fugues.py, solve_counterpoint() selects a triad at
  every quarter-note position and confines every free pitch to that triad.
  Its objective favors proximity to one shared contour,
  [0,2,3,2,0,-2,-3,-2,0,3,2,0], and inexpensive melodic motion.

- FugueBuilder uses essentially one construction for the cycle: successive
  small-group expositions, related-key entries, a short overlap, a full
  ensemble section, return and cadence. Changing the key, seed, opening
  scale degrees and tempo does not change this underlying rhetoric.

- The subjects were deliberately given a sustained terminal tonic so the
  same minimal overlap would work. That design convenience removes precisely
  the forward pressure a good subject can create.

- dense_episode() writes three parts first. dense_subject_episode() writes
  four. The remaining lines are inserted into their fixed harmony, with a
  minimum number of pitch changes as a proxy for independence.

- The dense revision still retains three or four "core" parts, then fills
  the missing voices. Its new development again prescribes a common
  progression and projects a short subject contour into its chord tones.

- Rhythmic refinement comes after these structural decisions. It preserves
  the anchors and inserts permitted local figures.

The result can pass increasingly sophisticated error screens while retaining
the same musical personality. The voices added last are structurally
subordinate by construction. Renaming their nearest available chord tones
"derived counterpoint" does not change that.

A DIFFERENT CONSTRUCTION ORDER

1. Stop treating 24 movements as a batch.

There should be one compositional lead and a sequential cycle of write,
render, listen, then reject or revise. Independent agents can expose defects
or investigate possibilities; they should not independently complete
movements whose aesthetic decisions have never been heard together.

The immediate problem should be one convincing nine-part paragraph, followed
by its complete fugue. The new two-part I is only a small audition candidate.
Its existence does not validate a scalable method.

2. Design the difficult combination before writing a complete movement.

For the nine-part work, write a short list of specific musical events that
the eventual convergence must accomplish. For example:

- A subject enters while an earlier statement is still moving.
- Its entry changes which line controls the bass.
- One companion's delayed resolution crosses the next entry.
- A previously heard fragment becomes the continuation that carries the
  sequence to a new tonal arrival.
- The final arrival requires the participating lines to finish at different
  moments.

Then write the actual subject and companion phrases needed for that
particular situation. Do not decide on nine generic active tracks and fill
them.

This is a legitimate place to fail early. A beautiful isolated subject may
have insufficient contrapuntal possibilities for this project. Conversely,
a subject that admits dozens of legal overlays may be musically inert.

3. Compose the nine-line paragraph as a coupled problem.

A two- or three-line trial is useful for learning how a subject behaves. It
must remain revisable when the fourth, fifth and later parts enter. Do not
freeze it as an already complete composition with empty slots around it.

Before completing pitches, give each of the nine continuous lines an actual
phrase trajectory: entry, continuation, interruption or suspension, response,
and destination. These trajectories should cross the proposed section
boundaries.

As each voice is written, revise any earlier line whose interval function or
melodic destination changes. The added voice must be allowed to alter the
contrapuntal argument. That is the crucial difference from insertion into
fixed harmony. All parts remain revisable throughout this process.

The target is not nine simultaneous complete subjects. Free counterpoint is
legitimate. But its phrases should be composed in response to the thematic
events, not selected from the chord currently underneath them.

4. Build the fugue around that paragraph only after it works.

Compose the exposition to teach the relationships the later combination will
transform. Compose episodes from the actual difficulty of moving from one
combination to the next: a fragment extends, a cadence is deferred, a bass
entry displaces another line, a sequence carries the continuation elsewhere.

A broad tonal plan is useful. A predetermined chord at every beat is
premature. Harmony should constrain and emerge from the authored lines
together.

5. Treat 25 parts as a new compositional problem.

A successful nine-part fugue would not justify adding sixteen parts to it.

For 25, investigate a multiple-subject design with contrasting rates of
motion, conceived for the full ensemble from the start. Slower thematic
lines, active subjects and shorter responses can coexist, with their
functions exchanged over time. No group should remain permanent harmonic
padding.

Spatial choirs could introduce material, but their eventual convergence must
involve new cross-group contrapuntal relationships. Five simultaneous
self-contained five-part fugues would not by itself solve the user's
challenge.

Before attempting the whole work, the necessary evidence would be a
sustained 25-line convergence that moves through a real harmonic and
thematic development. A static tonic field is not that evidence.

WHAT ALGORITHMS CAN USEFULLY DO

The useful search object is a placement or transformation of an authored
phrase, not an individual chord tone.

Given actual phrases, software can enumerate entry intervals, offsets,
registral permutations and specified augmentations; then identify precisely
where each combination fails. A result might say: "This entry distance
produces an unprepared seventh in bar 3; postponing the second entry destroys
the intended suspension."

For nine voices there are 36 voice pairs; for 25 there are 300. Checking
these relationships is worthwhile. But pairwise compatibility is
insufficient: a new lowest part can change the dissonance status of
previously compatible upper parts. The program therefore also needs actual
bass context, metrical position, preparation, continuation and resolution.

Useful outputs would include:

- Compatible and incompatible phrase combinations, with exact offending
  events.
- Unresolved directed notes and failed continuations.
- The consequences of exchanging two voices' registral positions.
- Passages that reduce to prolonged doubling or static support.
- Alternative placements of already authored material.

The search must be allowed to return no satisfactory combination. It must
not quietly repair failure by holding common tones, changing all difficult
notes into chord tones or thinning the music back to three parts.

None of these outputs chooses the most compelling phrase.

CONCRETE EXAMPLE: AN ADDED VOICE CHANGES THE COUNTERPOINT

Consider an initial two-line plan:

Beat 1: Upper E4, held into beat 2; lower C4.
Beat 2: Upper E4 continues; lower A3.
Beat 3: Upper C4; lower E3.

The vertical intervals are a third, fifth and sixth.

Now introduce a new lowest line A2-F2-F2. On beat 2, the already sounding E4
becomes a prepared seventh above F2. The old leap E4-C4 no longer supplies
the intended resolution, and the proposed E3 on beat 3 introduces another
seventh above F2.

A coherent revision is:

Beat 1: Upper E4, held into beat 2; middle C4; new bass A2.
Beat 2: Upper E4 continues; middle A3, held into beat 3; new bass F2, held
        into beat 3.
Beat 3: Upper D4; middle A3 continues; new bass F2 continues.

The new bass creates a 7-6 suspension; the upper line must resolve E4-D4.
The fourth A3-D4 between the upper parts is supported by the actual bass.
Adding the third voice changes the function and continuation of both
existing lines.

An insertion routine could avoid all this by choosing a safer bass note.
That would miss the musical opportunity.

FAILURE CONDITIONS

The new method has failed if:

- The many-part passage depends on long static harmony merely to accommodate
  its voice count.
- Removing most voices leaves every significant thematic exchange intact,
  with the discarded parts supplying only thickness.
- Added voices cannot force any reconsideration of the earlier lines.
- Rhythmic energy still has to be applied afterward as ornament.
- The subjects are interchangeable between movements without fundamentally
  changing their forms.
- The dry, plainly balanced rendering is unconvincing and needs orchestral
  color or reverberation to suggest importance.
- We cannot identify which concrete musical event makes the convergence more
  compelling than the preceding, thinner texture.

Those are reasons to reject and recompose the material, not reasons to
increase the solver budget or strengthen the success language.
