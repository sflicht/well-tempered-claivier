THE WELL-TEMPERED clAIvier — RECOMPOSITION BRIEF

Sam's correction, 22 October 2026

The second edition does not satisfy the artistic brief. Its 25-staff finale
does not establish 25-part counterpoint. The work also lacks the rhythmic zest
and convincing musical development required even of the earliest pieces.

The cycle remains 24 preludes and fugues through the major and minor keys,
with N+1 actual contrapuntal parts in Fugue N. Increasing the number of parts
should open increasingly intense expressive possibilities. Sam's intended
destination is an almost religious ecstasy earned by the music. The early
two-part work must already sustain a complete, compelling musical argument.

POSITIVE LISTENING REFERENCES

Sam likes Prelude III and Prelude VIII and hears promise in them. Preserve
these as positive reference pieces while inspecting what makes their musical
writing cohere. Do not assume that the whole cycle requires replacement.
Sam also likes Fugue VIII's climactic harmonic arrival, while finding that
its nine-part texture remains harmony rather than nine-part counterpoint.
Preserve the useful harmonic destination when rebuilding its moving lines.

Sam's later listening assessment is broader: the preludes are generally more
promising, the fugues sound too similar, and the approach to many-part fugues
must be rethought from scratch. Retain the preludes for selective development.
Retire the shared fugue-generation and harmonic-insertion method. Do not
assemble another complete cycle by ornamenting or extending that method.

The Beethoven reference supplied by Sam concerns the creative use of fugal
passages within symphonic arguments. Take from it a demand for a different
dramatic purpose and formal course in each work: fugal writing can gather,
interrupt, destabilize, transform or reconcile material. This is inspiration
for musical form; it does not relax the required reality of the N+1 parts.

Sam has granted broad creative freedom to define an AI Fugue genre. Original
tunes, borrowed melodies, nonclassical influences, recycled AI-generated
material and cheeky Bach/Beethoven allusions are all optional possibilities.
Do not convert these permissions into requirements. Take responsibility for
piece-specific musical choices, permit playfulness and risk, and avoid
turning the project into another uniformly solemn exercise.

WHAT MUST CHANGE

Compose the actual gestures and their consequences. A subject needs a
recognizable character, a metrical shape and useful harmonic implications.
Its companion must contribute a continuing line. Each episode must have
a reason to arise from the preceding material and to lead where it leads.

Write the joins between phrases as carefully as the phrases themselves.
An answer should change the situation. A cadence should resolve a tension
that the preceding lines created. A rising register, a louder rendering or
an expressive section title cannot supply that missing history.

Rhythmic zest is not a quota of short notes or offbeat attacks. It depends on
impulse, accent, contour, repetition, exchange, harmonic pace and destination.
An even stream of notes can have strong propulsion. Irregular notes can be
inert. Do not optimize a numerical rhythm-variety score as a proxy for drive.

For the large fugues, compose the intended contrapuntal combination before
committing to its exposition. Establish particular themes, countersubjects,
inversions and entry distances that actually work together. Added parts must
carry coherent continuations and create new relationships. Chord doubling,
octave relocation and the distribution of one role among more staves do not
fulfill the additional-voice requirement.

Use checks to find specific errors in composed material. Do not let a solver
select generic chord tones and then mistake the resulting admissibility for
composition. A clean audit cannot certify a fugue, independence, beauty or
the desired emotional effect.

CURRENT WORK

Two fresh, deliberately authored studies: Prelude IV in C-sharp minor and
Fugue I in C major. Their source notes, piano demonstrations and scores are
provided for musical assessment. They are not a third completed edition.
The previous 48-work edition is retained as an unsuccessful historical draft.
Its fugues are not accepted as the compositional foundation of the reset.
No revised nine-part or twenty-five-part fugue has been completed. The two-part
study supplies no evidence that a successful method scales to larger forces.

REVIEW STANDARD

Inspect the identifiable gesture, phrase connections, bass direction,
exchange of initiative, use of related keys and the necessity of the return.
Check actual dissonances and melodic joins without flattening intentional
passing motion or suspensions. Then judge the recordings perceptually.

Direct audio input is unsupported in the current assistant session. Score
inspection and successful playback decoding must not be described as having
listened to the music. The user's listening feedback is an essential part
of the present assessment, not a conclusion supplied by technical checks.
